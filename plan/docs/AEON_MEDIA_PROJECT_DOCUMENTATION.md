# AEON Media — Brand & Portfolio Website Project Documentation

**Document type:** Brand foundation and website project brief  
**Brand:** AEON Media  
**Project:** Premium portfolio website  
**Status:** Direction established; website implementation and final content remain in progress  
**Primary market context:** India-based creative company serving brands; international opportunities may be explored as the portfolio develops.

---

## 1. Project Overview

AEON Media is a creative company that helps brands communicate their value and build a stronger digital presence through creative direction, content planning, production, social media management, editing, post-production, and paid social support.

The portfolio website must do more than list services. It must help a prospective client understand what they gain by working with AEON Media: a clearer brand presence, better product presentation, more consistent content, less day-to-day creative coordination, and a considered approach to reaching the right audience.

The website should make a visitor feel that AEON Media understands the client's vision, takes the work seriously, communicates clearly, and can be trusted to carry the work from planning through execution.

### The central principle

> **Lead with the value the client receives. Use services, process, and project evidence to prove how that value is delivered.**

Prospective clients are primarily interested in their own business, brand, time, audience, and results. They do not need a long explanation of every internal task AEON Media performs. Explain the benefit first, then show the capability and process that support it.

## 2. Brand Foundation

### 2.1 Brand name

**AEON Media**

Use this spelling and capitalisation consistently. In visual wordmarks, use uppercase: **AEON MEDIA**.

### 2.2 Brand category and positioning

AEON Media is a **creative company** and a creative partner to brands. Do not describe AEON Media using any alternative category that the user has expressly rejected. In particular, do not use the disallowed A-word category in website copy, metadata, documents, social posts, or client communications.

### 2.3 Vision

To become a trusted creative partner for ambitious brands, recognised for understanding each brand's vision and translating it into a clear, consistent, and compelling digital presence.

### 2.4 Mission

To understand each client's business, audience, identity, and goals; combine that understanding with AEON Media's creative experience; and deliver thoughtful planning, content, production, social media management, and marketing support that helps the brand move forward.

### 2.5 Purpose

To close the gap between how a business sees its own value and how clearly that value is communicated to its audience.

### 2.6 Brand promise

**We understand your vision, take ownership of the creative work, and work with you to bring that vision to life.**

This is a commitment to understanding, care, communication, and quality of execution. It is not a guarantee of specific sales, follower counts, leads, virality, or financial performance.

### 2.7 Core positioning statement

AEON Media is a creative company for brands that want a more considered, consistent, and compelling digital presence. We bring together creative direction, content planning, production, social media management, editing, post-production, and paid social support—guided by the client's vision and business objectives.

### 2.8 Brand personality

The brand should feel:

- **Trustworthy:** dependable, responsible, clear, and considered.
- **Premium:** understated, spacious, polished, and intentional.
- **Creative:** visually aware, thoughtful, and original.
- **Collaborative:** listens to the client and respects the client's vision.
- **Capable:** organised, experienced, and confident without exaggerated claims.
- **Approachable:** professional but human; clear rather than formal or corporate.

### 2.9 Brand values

1. **Understanding before execution** — learn the client's business, audience, preferences, and direction before proposing content.
2. **Shared vision** — combine the client's vision with AEON Media's creative experience rather than imposing a one-size-fits-all style.
3. **Ownership** — take the agreed work seriously and handle execution with care.
4. **Consistency** — give the client a clear plan and a dependable content process.
5. **Purposeful creativity** — create work that supports the brand's identity and communication goals.
6. **Value over activity** — judge the usefulness of the work by what it contributes to the client's brand, not by how many tasks can be listed.
7. **Honesty** — show real work, accurate results, and genuine client feedback only.

## 5. Services and Delivery Capabilities

Services should be described as the means by which the client receives value. The following are the capabilities currently identified by the user.

### 5.1 Social media management

**Client value:** A more consistent and thoughtfully managed digital presence, without the client needing to coordinate every content detail alone.

Activities may include:

- Understanding the brand, goals, audience, and preferences.
- Discussing references and creative direction with the client.
- Researching suitable content references from platforms such as Instagram and Pinterest.
- Preparing a content calendar.
- Planning content requirements and shoot needs.
- Coordinating content preparation and publishing support.
- Advising on posting cadence and content consistency.

Do not suggest that a fixed posting frequency, response-time commitment, community-management scope, or reporting package exists until the user confirms it.

### 5.2 Creative direction and content planning

**Client value:** A clearer idea of what the brand should publish and how its content can remain aligned with its identity.

The process begins with the client's vision and examples. AEON Media studies the references, considers the brand's niche and audience, and develops content directions that can be used in the calendar and production plan.

The client may share an idea they want recreated. AEON Media should understand the reference and adapt the concept to the client's brand rather than copying another creator's work without thought or permission.

### 5.3 Shoots and production

**Client value:** More considered product and brand visuals, created with a clear plan rather than improvised on the shoot day.

The content calendar and selected references help guide the shoot. The exact production scope—location, crew, equipment, styling, number of deliverables, and revisions—must be agreed with each client and must not be invented in the portfolio copy.

### 5.4 Video editing and post-production

**Client value:** Finished content that presents the brand professionally and communicates the intended message clearly.

Show actual editing examples, formats, and before/after comparisons when available. Do not claim turnaround times or revision limits without confirmed operating terms.

### 5.5 Meta Ads and Instagram advertising support

**Client value:** A way to extend the reach of suitable content and create additional opportunities to attract relevant viewers or enquiries.

The process described by the user is:

1. Build a usable content library.
2. Review the available content and select suitable pieces for promotion.
3. Boost or advertise selected content through Meta advertising tools when appropriate.
4. Aim to reach new viewers and relevant audiences.
5. Review performance against the client's agreed objective.

Do not imply that every post should be boosted. Do not promise a particular cost per lead, return on ad spend, sales result, or audience size. Ad spend, campaign management fees, account ownership, tracking setup, and reporting expectations must be clarified in the service agreement.

## 6. The AEON Media Client Experience

The following is the recommended presentation of the working approach, based on the process described by the user. It is a communication framework, not a claim that every project has identical stages.

### Stage 1 — Understand the brand

Understand the client's business, products, goals, audience, preferences, and vision. Ask for existing references and examples of the kind of content the client likes.

### Stage 2 — Research and creative direction

Explore relevant references and content formats. Assess how a concept can be adapted to the brand's identity and audience. Combine the client's direction with AEON Media's creative input.

### Stage 3 — Plan the content

Organise approved ideas into a content calendar. Make the planned content, reference, and production requirements clear.

### Stage 4 — Prepare and create

Coordinate the agreed shoot or content-production requirements. Keep the intended creative direction visible during execution.

### Stage 5 — Edit and prepare for publishing

Complete editing and post-production according to the agreed deliverables. Prepare content for the intended platforms and formats.

### Stage 6 — Publish and extend reach

Support the agreed publishing process. Where appropriate, identify suitable content for paid promotion and Meta advertising.

### Stage 7 — Review and improve

Review the work against the agreed objective. Use actual performance data and client feedback to guide subsequent content decisions.

## 7. Trust and Credibility Strategy

Trust must be earned through evidence, clarity, and a professional experience—not through inflated adjectives.

### 7.1 Proof to show

Prioritise:

- Real project images and videos.
- Actual brand work, with permission to display it.
- Clear project context and the client's original requirement.
- The creative challenge and the approach taken.
- Actual deliverables.
- Verified performance results, if available and approved for publication.
- Genuine client testimonials, used with permission.
- Behind-the-scenes production material where it demonstrates real work.
- Clear enquiry and communication routes.

### 7.2 Case-study evidence format

For each project, collect:

1. Client/brand name and permission status.
2. Industry or category.
3. Client objective or challenge.
4. What the client wanted to achieve.
5. Creative direction or solution.
6. Work completed and deliverables.
7. Final images/videos or links.
8. Measurable outcome, only if supported by source data.
9. Client feedback, only if genuine and approved.

If a result is not available, describe the work and deliverables accurately. Never make up numbers, client quotes, logos, awards, years of experience, or campaign outcomes.

### 7.3 Trust-building language

Prefer:

- “Built around your brand and your goals.”
- “A clear plan from idea to execution.”
- “Creative direction shaped with you, not imposed on you.”
- “A more consistent presence, managed with care.”
- “Thoughtful content, purposeful execution.”

Avoid:

- “Guaranteed viral growth.”
- “Guaranteed sales.”
- “The number one creative company.”
- “We make every business successful.”
- Any unverified client count, performance figure, certification, award, or testimonial.

## 8. Brand Voice and Copywriting Rules

### 8.1 Voice

Confident, warm, considered, direct, premium, and human.

### 8.2 Writing style

- Use plain, natural English.
- Prefer short, meaningful sentences.
- Speak to the client and the value they receive.
- Be confident without boasting.
- Avoid jargon unless it makes a technical point clearer.
- Use “we” when describing responsibility and “you/your brand” when explaining client value.
- Do not overuse words such as “innovative,” “revolutionary,” “world-class,” or “seamless.”

### 8.3 Benefit-first formula

Use this sequence for each major section:

1. **Client benefit:** What gets better for the client?
2. **Relevance:** Why does that matter to their brand or business?
3. **Evidence/capability:** What work, process, or example supports the claim?
4. **Action:** What should the visitor do next?

Example:

- Service-first: “We create content calendars.”
- Benefit-first: “Know what your brand is publishing next—and why. A considered content calendar gives your team a clearer plan for upcoming content, references, and shoots.”

### 8.4 Working headline direction

Preferred direction:

**Your vision, brought to life with purpose.**

Supporting message:

“Creative direction, content, and social media support shaped around your brand—so your digital presence feels more considered, consistent, and true to what you are building.”

This is a recommended working direction. Claude may develop alternatives, but must preserve the core idea and must not present an unapproved alternative as final.

## 9. Visual Identity Summary

The visual direction is understated luxury: spacious, editorial, warm, minimal, and confident.

### 9.1 Approved palette direction

- Warm Ivory — `#F8F6F1`
- Champagne Beige — `#E9E0D2`
- Muted Taupe — `#C9B99F`
- Deep Charcoal — `#292722`
- Pure White — `#FFFFFF` as a supporting surface colour

Detailed roles and tokens are defined in `AEON_MEDIA_COLOR_PALETTE.md` and `assets/aeon-media-theme.css`.

### 9.2 Visual principles

- Make the page feel spacious; whitespace is a design feature.
- Use a restrained colour system; do not add bright accent colours without approval.
- Prioritise photography and actual work as the main visual interest.
- Use editorial typography and strong visual hierarchy.
- Prefer thin borders and subtle tonal separation over heavy shadows.
- Avoid busy card grids, excessive rounded boxes, decorative gradients, and unnecessary ornaments.
- Use motion to guide attention, not to entertain at the expense of clarity.

### 9.3 Wordmark concept — ONME highlight

The brand name remains **AEON MEDIA**. Do not rename it, concatenate it into a different written brand name, or create a separate “ON ME” tagline in place of the wordmark.

The visual signature is a single-line wordmark in which the sequence **ONME** is highlighted by one rounded rectangular background element:

- “ON” are the final two letters of “AEON”.
- “ME” are the first two letters of “MEDIA”.
- The highlight spans those four letters and the normal inter-word gap between “ON” and “ME”.
- “AE” remains outside the highlight on the left; “DIA” remains outside on the right.
- All letters remain on one baseline with consistent type size and tracking.
- The highlight is behind the letters, not an underline and not a separate badge placed beside the name.
- Default digital treatment: Deep Charcoal highlight with Warm Ivory letters for strong contrast; an approved softer option is Champagne Beige with Deep Charcoal letters.
- Preserve generous internal padding around the highlighted sequence, with softly rounded corners.
- Use the supplied reference image at `assets/aeon-media-wordmark-reference.png` to understand the position and proportions.

The ONME treatment is a typographic brand signature. It may quietly reinforce reliability and ownership, but the site should not add an explanation beside the logo every time it appears.

## 11. Website Structure — Agreed Starting Recommendation

Build **five main public pages**, plus a repeatable project-detail template:

1. **Home** — immediate positioning, client value, selected work, industries, process, and enquiry action.
2. **Work** — portfolio gallery and selected projects.

## 12. Creative and Technical Implementation Principles

- Build a responsive website with desktop, tablet, and mobile layouts designed intentionally.
- Use semantic structure and accessible controls.
- Keep the implementation modular, typed, and easy to maintain.
- Use reusable components for repeated patterns.
- Keep content separate from presentational components wherever practical.
- Use optimised image formats and responsive image sizes.
- Use server-rendered/static content by default; add client-side interaction only where it improves the experience.
- Use the available MCP tools to discover suitable components, design references, documentation, and assets when substantial implementation work is requested. Follow the automatic tool-use rules in `CLAUDE.md` and `.claude/rules/mcp-tool-orchestration.md`.
- Do not install dependencies or introduce complex infrastructure without a clear need.

The recommended implementation baseline is Next.js with TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and a lightweight motion library. Check the existing repository before choosing or changing versions, frameworks, and dependencies.
